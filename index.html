Abstract

High-definition maps provide critical structural information for downstream autonomous driving tasks. Recent methods increasingly rely on external priors to improve prediction accuracy and robustness. Yet, existing prior representations expose a trade-off: dense rasters are easy to integrate but ignore certain map geometric properties, while sparse vectors preserve geometric structure but require dedicated encoders and are difficult to plug into existing architectures. Moreover, external priors can be inaccurate, costly to acquire, or difficult to maintain, motivating a structural representation that can provide geometric guidance while remaining learnable and broadly applicable. We address this limitation with the Lane Field, a dense BEV-aligned geometric representation that encodes local lane structure at each spatial location and can be either predicted from onboard observations or constructed from external map priors. Building on this representation, we introduce a plug-and-play framework that enhances online HD mapping through two complementary mechanisms: BEV feature modulation, which injects lane-structured cues into latent BEV features, and deformable attention guidance, which steers query sampling toward the underlying lane geometry. We primarily evaluate our method on OpenLane-V2, showing that conditioning BEV features and guiding deformable attention with local lane geometry enables our framework to consistently improve existing methods and achieve state-of-the-art performance on lane segment perception.

Overview

A dense field for proximity, orientation and confidence. Lane Field turns local lane geometry into a conditioning signal for BEV features and deformable attention.

Method

Camera observations → BEV features → Lane Field prediction → feature modulation and guided attention. Vector annotations provide analytic supervision during training; external map priors can supply the same representation.

1. Spread each lane segment over the grid

Split each centerline into straight segments. Segment i has endpoints Ai and Bi, length ℓi, unit tangent t̂i = (Bi − Ai) / ℓi, and average lane width w̄i. Its Gaussian spread is σi = f · w̄i.

Ii(p)=ℓi ∫01 exp⁡(− ‖p−Ai−t(Bi−Ai)‖2 2σi2 )dt

Here, t moves from one endpoint to the other. Integrating the Gaussian along the finite segment gives a smooth contribution Ii(p): nearby locations receive more support, and distant locations receive less. The factor f controls how broadly that support spreads.

2. Accumulate direction and proximity

At each grid location, sum the contributions of all segments. Weighting each tangent’s outer product produces a symmetric 2 × 2 directional tensor M; summing the weights alone gives the proximity P.

M(p)=∑iIi(p)t^it^i⊤
P(p)=∑iIi(p)

The outer product is unchanged when a tangent is reversed, so the tensor describes a lane’s axis rather than its travel direction. Proximity measures how strongly nearby lane geometry supports that location.

3. Measure directional agreement

Let λ1 ≥ λ2 be the eigenvalues of M(p). Their relative difference defines the direction confidence C: it is high when a single axis dominates and lower when competing orientations contribute, as at an intersection.

C(p)=λ1(p)−λ2(p)λ1(p)+λ2(p)

The resulting representation has five channels at each BEV location: three tensor entries, proximity, and directional confidence.

R(p)=[Mxx(p),Mxy(p),Myy(p),P(p),C(p)]

The demo evaluates the closed-form integral from the supplement: Ii(p) = σi√(π/2) exp(−d⊥,i² / 2σi²) [erf(u1,i) − erf(u0,i)], where ai = (p − Ai)⊤t̂i, u0,i = −ai / (√2 σi) and u1,i = (ℓi − ai) / (√2 σi). The projection onto the supporting line is not clamped; the integration bounds account for finite endpoints.

For training targets, Pgt = P / (P + 1) and m = sigmoid(20(Pgt − 0.3)). The output tensor is Mgt = M / (tr(M) + ε) · m, and Cgt = C · m. The five live outputs above use these transformations; the matrix and eigenvalue readouts show the raw accumulations. The implementation also uses a minimum Gaussian spread and numerical stabilization. These constructed fields supervise the prediction head and can also encode external map priors.

How wide should the field spread?

The demo keeps σ fixed. In the paper, σi = f · w̄i adapts it to lane width. Compare the actual ground-truth fields below: increasing f broadens support. The paper uses f = 0.50.
